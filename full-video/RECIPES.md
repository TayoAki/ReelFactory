# Teaching and motion recipes



These are editorial interpretations grounded in the clocked source study. The native reconstruction is a review artifact, not proof of exact source fidelity. For a new technical script, select by the relationship being taught and verify product claims before creating assets.



## opening — presenter

**Relationship:** The speaker frames or reflects on the explanation.

**Why:** A deliberate presenter hold allows the narration to frame the next visual.

**Motion:** Static fictional presenter placeholder; short callout only when explicitly named in the source.

**Keep stable:** Presenter and framing layout.

**Alternative:** Continuous decorative motion. Motion would compete with framing language rather than explain it.

**Misuse risk:** Static placeholders do not imply lip sync or endorsement by a real speaker.



## intro-map — convergence

**Relationship:** Several sources feed one context or query route.

**Why:** A labeled convergence map makes the source-to-consumer relationship explicit while preserving source identity.

**Motion:** Reveal sources first; draw their connector paths next; reveal or activate the destination last. Hold the completed route.

**Keep stable:** Keep source labels and the destination fixed after the build.

**Alternative:** A list of connector logos. A list names sources but does not explain how they participate in the answer.

**Misuse risk:** Converging arrows can wrongly imply ingestion, copying, or real-time synchronization. Label the access mode: search, SQL, or tool call.

## product-family — family-comparison

**Relationship:** Related products share foundations but serve different jobs.

**Why:** Depth establishes a shared family before peer cards separate the roles.

**Motion:** Preserve the front card; move hidden peers out from behind it; retain readable card planes during the spread.

**Keep stable:** Front-card scale and independent presenter tile.

**Alternative:** An execution pipeline. A pipeline would imply that one product executes another.

**Misuse risk:** Depth must not imply an architectural dependency.

## wrong-source — failure-repair

**Relationship:** The same question produces a wrong answer when its source or definition is missing.

**Why:** A concrete question and visible error isolate the cause before adding the missing context.

**Motion:** Establish the question-to-answer route; expose the wrong choice; add the specific source or rule that repairs that route.

**Keep stable:** Keep the question and outcome visible across the repair.

**Alternative:** An abstract ontology network. A network cannot identify which missing definition caused this particular error.

**Misuse risk:** Do not imply that every error is solved by adding context; permissions, data quality and model behavior may also matter.

## architecture — architecture-focus

**Relationship:** A component belongs to a larger system and must be examined in place.

**Why:** A stable overview gives the viewer a location before attention shifts to a sub-system.

**Motion:** Start at the complete map; dim context selectively; move the inner camera or enlarge only the active region; hold the readable detail.

**Keep stable:** Fixed outer stage, presenter, component names and relative relationships.

**Alternative:** Replace the overview with an unrelated full-screen detail slide. The viewer loses the connection between the detail and the architecture.

**Misuse risk:** A camera move must not resize the presenter or push the active labels outside the panel.



## modeled — governed-hierarchy

**Relationship:** Metric definitions, explanatory pages and domains share a governance parent.

**Why:** Containment and parent-child connectors express organization more precisely than decorative data nodes.

**Motion:** Introduce the first governed object, add its parent, expose peer objects, then show the group and downstream consumers.

**Keep stable:** Shared parent and the first object remain visible as peers are introduced.

**Alternative:** Three floating feature cards. Equal unconnected cards omit the shared governing layer and grouping relationship.

**Misuse risk:** Visual nesting must correspond to an actual product relationship; do not invent inheritance or access semantics.



## learned — evidence-accumulation

**Relationship:** Repeated work and concrete sources provide contextual signals.

**Why:** Small labeled examples show where context originates before returning to the larger abstraction.

**Motion:** Show a small source set; accumulate examples sequentially; keep completed examples still.

**Keep stable:** The learned-context label and one current example group.

**Alternative:** An unlabeled glowing knowledge graph. A glowing network shows complexity but does not teach which behavior supplies useful context.

**Misuse risk:** Do not suggest that a signal is certified truth or that every user action automatically creates a governed definition.

## benchmark — benchmark

**Relationship:** Multiple methods are compared on accuracy and runtime.

**Why:** Paired bar charts expose the two trade-offs and keep the measured values visible.

**Motion:** Build bars from a common baseline; introduce the interpretation after the values; reuse the same chart during the follow-up.

**Keep stable:** Axes, values, method order and source qualification.

**Alternative:** A single improvement percentage. A single number hides runtime and the comparator values.

**Misuse risk:** Treat source-reported measurements as historical claims. Do not generalize them to a new product or workload.

## demo-one-title — chapter

**Keep stable:** Concise title and consistent chapter treatment.

## governance — peer-needs

**Relationship:** Several needs or stages have equal conceptual rank.

**Why:** Aligned peer cards give each need equal weight and make comparison easy.

**Motion:** Settle the first card, compress it coherently if space is needed, then introduce equal peers on the same baseline.

**Keep stable:** Shared card height, type hierarchy and reading order.

**Alternative:** A nested hierarchy. Nesting would imply that one peer is a subtype or permission parent of another.

**Misuse risk:** Use sequence numbers only when order matters. Three items alone is not a reason to use cards.

## rollout-one — rollout

**Relationship:** A team follows an ordered implementation process.

**Why:** A persistent milestone rail shows both the current task and its place in the rollout.

**Motion:** Retain completed milestones; add the next marker; update supporting examples below; pan only when the rail needs space.

**Keep stable:** Milestone order and active-step treatment.

**Alternative:** Independent full-screen checklist slides. Separate slides lose accumulated progress and the relationship between steps.

**Misuse risk:** Small faded text must remain legible; a timeline is not a promise about duration.

## best-practices — fixed-emphasis

**Relationship:** The narration walks through a fixed set of recommendations.

**Why:** Stable positions let the viewer scan the whole set while emphasis identifies the current point.

**Motion:** Keep all rows stationary; advance text emphasis at the spoken topic change; hold after each change.

**Keep stable:** Row positions, number order and line breaks.

**Alternative:** Replace the entire screen for each recommendation. Replacement forces the viewer to rebuild the list and obscures its total scope.

**Misuse risk:** Do not fade inactive rows below useful contrast or move the active row into a different position.

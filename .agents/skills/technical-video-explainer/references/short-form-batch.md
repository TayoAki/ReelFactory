# Long recording → source-faithful short-form batch

## Source and quantity

Ingest and inspect the actual uploaded recording before claiming extracted moments. Transcribe with word timings, check technical terms and speaker turns, then select complete takeaways with enough context. Keep the original audio. Do not replace it with TTS or an earlier written interview. If the recording cannot be accessed, prepare clearly labelled provisional drafts and report the exact blocker; keep every audio timestamp null.

Count distinct supported lessons, not overlapping cuts or aspect-ratio variants. Build as many self-contained shorts as the source supports; reject incomplete or repetitive cuts. Retain short source excerpts and citations for each exact spoken claim. Written titles are editorial summaries, not additional recorded speech. Never invent a spoken hook or CTA to satisfy a template.

## How the provided guides combine

- Short-Form Content Agent Playbook: specific takeaway; verbal/written/visual hooks with one promise; LINE/SHOT/EDIT plan; sources; QA. Hypothetical worked examples are not verified performance proof. Do not label a draft `proven` without observed data.
- Editor V1: device choice follows spoken meaning, source cards show evidence, enumerations reveal one item per word, timelines show actual chronology, and audio effects support intelligibility. Its reported frame recipes are source-package measurements, not our independent measurements.
- This project: keep interview roles, Fabric tokens, exact recorded wording, generated storyboard images, editable diagrams, readable holds and safe captions. Do not mechanically force the kit's fast cuts, red/amber colors, 16:9 layout or loud music onto every technical short.

## Production sequence

1. Accessible source → full ASR draft → checked quotations/speaker turns.
2. Moment bank: real source in/out, exact excerpt, practical takeaway, dependency/context caveats, overlap/variant labels.
3. Draft hook/title and visual recipe; no spoken additions. Use the best actual recorded sentence as the verbal hook when it remains truthful out of context.
4. Source subject evidence and choose a matching teaching relationship from our reference library. Authentic UI for execution; labelled illustrations for concepts.
5. Generate a master and three or more coherent state images; review and rebuild independent layers.
6. Recompose vertically for Shorts/Reels rather than cropping the 16:9 scene. Reserve platform UI margins and caption space; never cover the face or essential evidence.
7. Align graphics and captions to verified actual audio. Label static fictional portraits if using prior authorized placeholders with audio-only input.
8. Render supported clips. Check beginning/middle/end and every caption phrase, audio intelligibility, complete sentence boundaries, voice/role continuity, visual truthfulness and export properties.
9. Deliver actual MP4s, source-time citations, caption files, storyboard/asset references, a gallery and an honest status table. Draft, verified and rendered are separate states. No social posting without instructions.

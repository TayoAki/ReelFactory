# ReelFactory video standard

Derived from the Parker Prompts study in research/parker-prompts/STUDY.md and the first-draft comparison. The rules below are our production decisions; they are not claimed measurements of the whole channel.

## Start with the useful result

For a short reel, put an actual output, comparison or demonstrable failure in the first scene. For a longer tutorial, establish the problem and show a finished example before extended setup. A headline about the topic is not evidence that the promised result exists.

## Write a visual contract before animating

Lock the aspect ratio, typography, palette, data snapshot, names, numbers, chart scales, recurring layouts and source attribution. For a generated character or object, lock its reference views first. For a technical explainer, lock the dataset and the diagram's meaning first.

Use the existing V1 tokens for new technical graphics unless a separate visual brief says otherwise. Preserve the real colors and identity of any official brand mark. Do not copy a reference creator's face, voice, distinctive branded template or films.

## Give every scene a new information state

The shot list must say what changes: a result appears, a filter is applied, a comparison is made, an exact UI action happens, or a source claim is verified. A new crop or color grade of the same unreadable screenshot is not a new scene.

Carry stable elements across related shots. In a data story, keep category colors, chart geometry and the dataset identity consistent. Use actual numbers from retained responses. Do not simulate a vendor product execution that never happened.

## Let the evidence occupy the picture

Use large original data graphics, an actual application recording, a precise documentation crop, or a clearly attributed source excerpt. Reserve full-page screenshots for orientation. The relevant control, sentence or value must be readable at the intended viewing size. Preserve labels and axes when cropping charts.

For 1080-pixel-wide vertical data graphics, target primary numbers at least 100 px and normal chart labels around 28–36 px. These are working layout targets, not universal accessibility guarantees.

## Animate a controlled change

Approve representative stills before rendering. Keep labels and glyphs deterministic in HTML/SVG for technical graphics. Use transforms and opacity; keep the data geometry truthful throughout the stable portion of a shot. Avoid animating SVG scale origins in ways that move a chart's baseline.

Use cuts, focus, cursor emphasis and sound when the explanation changes. Do not swap crops on a fixed clock merely to make the timeline busier. Let complex evidence breathe. A generated cinematic shot may need a camera plan; a technical chart usually needs an attention plan.

## Keep the claim and the evidence together

Verify product claims against first-party sources. Treat a creator's model/pricing claims as claims until checked. Save query parameters, date windows and actual results for numerical stories. Distinguish request counts from incidents, screenshots from executions, and sampled visual reviews from exhaustive frame review.

If footage is unavailable, use a transcript only for narrative and workflow observations. Do not infer composition, transitions, sound levels or cursor behavior from text.

## Check a pilot before scaling

1. Read the script and per-scene evidence plan.
2. Inspect an opening, one representative action/result sequence, the most complex comparison, and the close as stills.
3. Repair readability, continuity and geometry problems before rendering.
4. Render a pilot with the actual V1/HyperFrames project.
5. Check final MP4 dimensions, frame rate, audio, duration and complete decode; inspect rendered frames and re-transcribe the final mix.
6. Keep ASR uncertainty visible rather than claiming a word-perfect mix from a high similarity score.

These are internal checks for reversible authorized work, not automatic permission gates. Do not batch-rewrite an entire finished library before the new treatment has produced a reviewable pilot. Do not publish unless the user has authorized publication.


## Fine-edit requirements from the Fabric revision

- Use genuine vendor motion when it demonstrates the named action. Retain the original GIF/video and its source; label separate demo sessions and preview-only features. Do not replace a missing app interaction with a fabricated recording.
- Establish the whole application before a detail. Crop only to a complete relevant control or panel; retain its title, input and buttons.
- Animate the screen wrapper into place from 98% to 100% scale. Keep the image itself at contain fit, so a zoom cannot cut off source text inside an overflow-hidden viewport.
- Time highlights to spoken controls. Keep one guided pointer active at a time; use outline highlights alone where overlapping pointer moves would distract.
- Reserve bands at 1920×1080: main content y190–900, provenance y902–934, captions y944–1008, progress rail y1018, attribution y1032. Keep content within x72–1848.
- Use smaller caption groups rather than forcing seven long words across a line. Verify visible word bounds and every scene layout.
- Review native snapshots before export, then sample the encoded final video. Check actual demo regions for visible frame changes, and transcribe the final sound mix to catch masked words. A sampled bounds audit is not an exhaustive human review of every frame.

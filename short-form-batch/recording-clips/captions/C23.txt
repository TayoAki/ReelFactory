The schema cannot explain priority
Original recording 22:49–23:55
ASR draft; technical-name spelling normalized.

A relational database might have a column named priority level, filled with the numbers one, two, and three. The schema knows it's an integer, but the schema has absolutely no idea what makes a one strategically different from a three in the real world. Which brings us to the most human element of this entire process, which is adding the metadata and the business roles. Right. This is where you inject the tacit tribal knowledge that actually determines how a business question is answered. It's like leaving highly detailed sticky notes all over a complex shared spreadsheet. So the next analyst who opens it doesn't accidentally delete the company's revenue projections because they didn't understand what a column meant. That is the exact mechanism. You have to manually codify the context that the schema simply cannot possess. Give me some real world examples of that. Well, in our retail operation scenario, this means explicitly writing out the definition of what constitutes a priority store. Is it based on square footage, total revenue, proximity to a distribution center, you have to define it. Right, because priority is totally subjective until you define it.

The billing specifics in the preview are fascinating and require extreme caution. Currently what Microsoft categorizes as the discovery and AI reasoning phases, the part where the LLM reads your prompt and figures out what you want, are non -billable during the preview phase. No, that sounds great. Free AI reasoning. But, and this is a massive trap for the unwary, the child operations that the AI triggers and the underlying OneLake storage compute it utilizes to formulate those answers can and will incur standard Fabric capacity charges. Walk me through how that traps springs. Imagine you have a non -technical manager who asks the chat interface a slightly vague massive question about five years of historical inventory across the entire globe. The AI reasoning to understand the question is free. But to answer it, the AI might quietly write a highly inefficient brute force SQL query that scans petabytes of data across your OneLake. It pushes that massive compute job down to the warehouse. And you pay for that compute. Suddenly, you aren't paying for the chat interaction. You are paying for an absolutely massive compute spike on your Fabric capacity bill, because the AI decided to brute force the answer. The source strongly recommends using the Fabric capacity metrics app to monitor this underlying compute consumption closely during any pilot program.

Inventory at risk has two definitions
Original recording 05:26–06:19
ASR draft; technical-name spelling normalized.

Inventory at risk is a brilliant example of these diagnostic muddy waters. Because it sounds so simple. It does. But if you go down to the warehouse and ask the manager what inventory at risk means, they will likely give you a purely physical definition. Meaning what? To them it means the physical count of stocks sitting on a pallet is currently below a hard minimum threshold at this exact second. Okay, but if you take that exact same phrase to a supply chain planner, the planner is going to give you a temporal predictive definition. Right, because their job is looking ahead. Exactly. To them, inventory at risk means that based on current sales velocity and historical trends, a predictive projection shows that the stock will fall below a required safety buffer at some point before the next delivery truck physically arrives. Those are two radically different mathematical equations. Completely different database queries triggered by the exact same three words.
